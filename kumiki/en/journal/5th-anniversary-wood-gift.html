The 5th Anniversary Wood Gift
She'll Actually Wear

2026.10.26 ｜ KUMIKI Journal

The traditional gift for a 5th wedding anniversary is wood. Most wooden gifts are things to look at — a frame, a box, a watch kept for special days. A piece of hinoki masu jewelry from Japan is a wood gift she can wear every day: handmade with nail-free joinery that has held Japanese temples together for 1,300 years, and quietly fragrant with the wood itself.

Why the fifth year is wood

The traditional anniversary list moves from fragile to enduring: paper for the first year, cotton for the second, leather for the third — and wood for the fifth. By five years a marriage has put down roots. Like a tree, it has weathered seasons and grown stronger for it. In France the same milestone is called noces de bois, the wood wedding.

That is why the best wood gift is not simply made of wood. It should carry the same idea: something rooted, something that improves with time, something meant to stay.

Beyond the wooden watch

Search for a wood anniversary gift and you will find the same few ideas: engraved frames, wooden watches, cutting boards, keepsake boxes. They are thoughtful, but most of them end up on a shelf. KUMIKI is different in three ways:

  • She wears it. Earrings, a necklace, or a kanzashi hair pin — the anniversary goes with her, not into a drawer.
  • It is genuinely crafted. Each masu is about 2cm wide with walls just 1.6mm thin, its corners joined by hand with arare-gumi — interlocking teeth of wood, no nails, no glue. Our atelier completes no more than ten pieces a month.
  • It is hinoki. Japanese cypress, the wood of shrines and temples. Its soft, clean scent remains even after the protective finish.

A vessel that means "to increase"

A masu is the small wooden box Japan has used for 1,300 years to measure rice and sake — and to toast at weddings. Its name sounds like the Japanese word for "to increase" (増す). To give a masu is to wish that what you share keeps growing: masu-masu, more and more.

For a fifth anniversary, there is no simpler way to say it. Five years in — and may it keep increasing.
